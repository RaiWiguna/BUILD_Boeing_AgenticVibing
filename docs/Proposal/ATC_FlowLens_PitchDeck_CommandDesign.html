Slide 2 - Problem

Controllers must make fast decisions while monitoring many moving parts at once.

ATC workload rises when traffic, voice communication, weather, runway crossing, vehicle movement, and coordination happen at the same time.

What must be monitored

Aircraft approaching, landing, departing, or taxiing; active runway status; airport vehicles; runway crossing requests; weather and visibility; radio calls; readbacks; flight strips; and unit coordination.

Why workload becomes unsafe

Critical information may be distributed across displays, voice radio, human memory, and procedure assumptions. When time-to-conflict is short, the full picture must be understood immediately.

The runway conflict gap

A landing or departing aircraft can conflict with a ground vehicle if clearance status, vehicle movement, and aircraft timing are not interpreted as one operational state.

The opportunity is not to replace controllers, but to help them connect runway risk faster when workload is already high.

Slide 4 - Current Systems and Gap

ATC already has important technologies, but the challenge is connecting them into one clear operational picture.

Existing ATC / ATM technologies

Radar, ADS-B, MLAT, surface surveillance, electronic flight strips, A-SMGCS, RIMCAS/RMCA, STCA/MTCD, CPDLC/Data Comm, AMAN/DMAN, TFDM, and SWIM.

Coverage and fragmentation reality

Advanced surface tools are valuable but expensive and uneven. Voice communication remains common in tower and ground operations, so clearance state and runway timing still require interpretation.

Existing data

Aircraft, vehicles, runway, clearance, weather, communication logs.

FlowLens layer

Time-to-conflict, workload score, event explanation, replay timeline.

Human decision

Controller remains in command. The tool supports awareness and training.

Sources: ICAO Global Runway Safety Action Plan; MITRE surface safety prototypes; SKYbrary RIMCAS; FAA AIM ASDE-X/ASSC.

Solution
One operational
risk view
Slide 5 - Solution

ATC FlowLens brings runway movement, clearance status, vehicle activity, communication, and workload risk into one dashboard.

The product does not automate ATC. It connects operational signals into a single timeline so teams can detect, explain, and replay runway risk.

Runway occupancy

Clear, occupied, crossing, takeoff, or inbound landing state.

Clearance state engine

Cleared to land, takeoff, hold short, cross, line up and wait, go around.

Time-to-conflict

Compares aircraft runway entry or touchdown time with vehicle clear time.

Communication timeline

Organizes tower, pilot, and vehicle messages into event history.

Workload indicator

Scores traffic, radio calls, clearances, vehicles, weather, and alerts.

Replay and explanation

Shows what happened, when it happened, and why an alert appeared.

Slide 7 - MVP Scope and Demo Plan

The MVP is feasible for a hackathon because it uses simulation, rule-based logic, and a focused runway scenario.

Hackathon MVP

One simulated airport layout, runway and taxiway, three to five aircraft, one to two vehicles, clearance logs, communication logs, weather or visibility status, conflict detection, workload scoring, and replay.

Why this is feasible

It avoids live ATC data, regulatory integration, and certification dependency while still showing a realistic safety logic and operational dashboard.

Why this is valuable

Training centers and airport safety teams need explainable scenarios, not only alarms. FlowLens becomes a reusable safety learning environment.

Slide 10 - Roadmap and Closing

ATC FlowLens starts as a feasible hackathon MVP and can grow into a broader aviation safety training platform.

Phase 1: Hackathon MVP

Simulated runway map, aircraft and vehicle movement, clearance log, time-to-conflict alert, workload indicator, and replay timeline.

Phase 2: Enhanced prototype

ATC-style speech-to-text, readback mismatch detection, scenario generator, multi-runway support, and richer workload model.

Phase 3: Future vision

Airport operation datasets, tower-ground training simulator, safety analytics dashboard, and research pathway toward certified support.

BUILD Indonesia can help validate the concept with mentors, aerospace expertise, and a stronger path from prototype to aviation safety product direction.

Final statement

ATC FlowLens is not trying to automate the controller. It gives controllers, trainers, and safety teams a clearer lens on runway flow, workload, and conflict risk when every second matters.
